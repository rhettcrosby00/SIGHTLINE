    Two sides, one warm-up

    Focus · Eyes, mind, breath

    Hold your phone.

    Breathing that settles you, eye drills that wake up how you read the play, and fast decisions that sharpen your reactions. Every answer is a tap, so speed and accuracy are measured.

    Move · React, cut, compete

    Set your phone down.

    Big signals tell you where to go, and you cut to the right cone. Reacting to a cue you can’t predict is closer to the game than running a pattern you already know.

    What’s inside

    • Game Day presets. Quick, Standard and Game Speed: 5 to 8 minutes, calm, and they end easy.
    • Training mode. Longer, harder sessions for non-game days, with a weekly streak.
    • Eye drills. Smooth pursuit, saccade jumps, peripheral flash, quiet eye and gaze stabilization.
    • Visual skills. Near-far focus, target capture and perception span.
    • Decisions. Color calls, go / no-go and Stroop, kept short so your brain is warm, not tired.
    • Mental skills. Breathing, imagery and a cue word of your own.
    • MOVE drills. Reactive cone drills with colors, arrows, numbers or sport calls, a decision layer, and planned 5-10-5, square and T-drills.
    • Team leaderboard. Every session scores points. See who’s putting in the reps.
    • Eye Check (beta). Optional camera feedback during eye drills. Video never leaves your phone.
    • Works with no signal. Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app, anywhere.

    Why it works

    • Short beats long on game day. Long, demanding mental work causes fatigue that can slow decisions and reactions. Game Ready never runs past 10 minutes.
    • Thinking while moving carries over best. Reacting to a signal you can’t predict, then moving, is closer to the game than screen-only drills.
    • The strongest evidence: breathing, pre-performance routines, quiet eye, imagery and self-talk.
    • Real gains take repetition. Most studies used 2 to 4 sessions a week for 4 weeks or more. One session is a warm-up, not a program.
    • Honest limits. Sightrep is a warm-up. It isn’t a vision, concussion or medical assessment, and it can’t promise better games.

    Research behind it: perceptual-cognitive training, mental fatigue, cyclic sighing, quiet eye, imagery. The full list is in the app, under About.
